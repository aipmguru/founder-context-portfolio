# goals.md

## What This File Is For

What you're optimizing for, across timeframes. This is what every strategic decision should serve. Different from constraints (what you can't or won't do) — these are the targets you're aiming at. Refreshed monthly minimum.

---

## Interview Protocol

*Instructions for the interview agent.*

**Multi-file capture:** Feeds `thesis-and-bets.md` (bets serve goals), `constraints.md` (goals plus constraints define the strategic frontier).

**Conversation flow:**

1. **This week.** "What are you optimizing for this week? One thing — the most important outcome by Friday."
   - If they list five things, push: "If you could only get one done, which one?"

2. **This month.** "Same question, one month out. What does success look like by [date]?"

3. **This quarter.** "90 days out. What's the milestone that matters most?"

4. **This year.** "12 months out. What's the company looking like? Revenue, customers, team, position?"

5. **Company-wide / long-term.** "Strip the time horizon away. What's the company optimizing for over the long arc? Revenue at exit? Mission impact? Lifestyle business that pays you well? Acquisition? Independence?"
   - This shapes everything. If they say "I haven't thought about it," that's the answer to capture — and a flag.

6. **What you're explicitly NOT optimizing for.** "What's the goal you've decided NOT to chase, even though everyone tells you to? The growth-at-all-costs you turned down, the feature you won't build, the segment you won't expand to?"

**Stop condition:** You have one specific goal per timeframe (week, month, quarter, year, long-term) with a measurable definition of success, plus at least one explicit "not chasing this" goal.

**Judgment-layer challenge:**
> *Coherence lens. Read goals back across timeframes. Ask: "Do these stack? Does the weekly goal serve the monthly? Does the monthly serve the quarterly? Or are you optimizing for different things at different timeframes — which usually means drift."*
