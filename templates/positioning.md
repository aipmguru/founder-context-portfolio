# positioning.md

## What This File Is For

Where you stand in the market and what you believe that others don't. Not a marketing one-liner. A position you hold — competitive frame, category, target customer, point of view. Built on the April Dunford 5-element model with a POV layer added.

---

## Interview Protocol

*Instructions for the interview agent.*

**Multi-file capture:** Pulls from `market-and-customers.md` (target customer), `competitive-landscape.md` (competitive alternatives), `founder-market-fit.md` (POV foundation).

**Conversation flow:**

1. **Competitive alternatives.** "When a buyer is choosing between you and the alternatives, what do they actually compare? Be specific — name the alternatives."
   - Note: this is the *buyer's* alternatives, not what *you* think your competitors are. Different list.

2. **Category.** "What category do you play in? Not the category you wish you were in. The category buyers put you in when they hear what you do."
   - If unclear, push: "If a buyer described you to a colleague in one sentence, what category would they say?"

3. **Unique value.** "What can your product do that the alternatives can't? Or what does it do meaningfully better?"
   - Push for specifics tied to outcomes, not features.

4. **Proof points.** "What's the evidence that backs your unique value claim? Customer outcomes, data, case studies, demos. What can you show?"
   - If thin, capture it as a gap to fill.

5. **Trend that makes this matter.** "What's happening in the world right now that makes this category matter more than it did 2 years ago? Why now?"

6. **The POV layer.** "What do you believe about this market that most people in it don't? Not facts — point of view. What's your contrarian take?"
   - This is where Strategic Positioning differs from generic positioning. Push for it.

**Stop condition:** You have all 5 Dunford elements + a stated POV that's actually contrarian (not a platitude).

**Judgment-layer challenge:**
> *Coherence lens. Read back the 5 elements + POV and ask: "Does this position cohere? Does the target customer match the alternatives they'd compare you to? Does the POV match the unique value? Flag any contradictions."*

# Positioning

## Competitive Alternatives
[What buyers actually compare you to. Named alternatives.]

## Category
[The category buyers put you in. Plain English.]

## Unique Value
[What you do that alternatives can't, or do meaningfully better. Outcome-tied, not feature-tied.]

## Proof Points
- [Evidence backing the unique value claim]
- [Evidence]
- [Evidence]

## Trend (Why Now)
[The shift in the world that makes this category matter now.]

## Point of View
[Your contrarian take on this market. The thing most people in this space don't believe that you do. One paragraph.]

## Target Customer
[The customer for whom this position is most valuable. Should match `market-and-customers.md` beachhead.]

## One-Liner (Output Artifact, Optional)
[If you want to compress all of the above into a single sentence for use in pitches, here it is. Generated last, not first.]

## Confidence
- Competitive alternatives: [Evidence / Faith / Untested]
- Unique value: [Evidence / Faith / Untested]
- Proof points: [Evidence / Faith / Untested]
- POV: [Evidence / Faith / Untested]

## Open Questions
[What you need to test or learn about your position.]

## Last Refreshed
[Date]
