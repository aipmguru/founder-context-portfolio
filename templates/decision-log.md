# decision-log.md

## What This File Is For

The history of strategic decisions you've made, the options you considered, your reasoning, and what would change your mind. NLW called this the most underrated file in his template. For founders, it's even more valuable — it lets every AI tool you use understand HOW you decide, not just WHAT you've decided.

This file gets richer over time. Add to it after every significant strategic call.

---

## Interview Protocol

*Instructions for the interview agent.*

**Multi-file capture:** Cross-references everything. Especially feeds `thesis-and-bets.md` (each bet was a decision) and `coherence-check` (decision history surfaces drift).

**Conversation flow:**

1. **How you make decisions.** "Walk me through your general approach to a strategic call. Do you list options? Talk to specific people? Sleep on it? Use a framework? What's your real process?"

2. **What you need before deciding.** "What information do you need before you'll commit? Customer evidence? Investor input? Cofounder agreement? A specific dollar threshold?"

3. **Walk me through the last 3 strategic decisions.** "I want specifics. Pick the three most important strategic decisions you've made in the last 6 months. For each: what was the question, what were the options, what did you choose, why, and what's the outcome so far (if known)?"
   - This is the core of the file. Push for at least 3 real decisions with real reasoning.
   - If they only have 1-2, capture those well rather than padding.

4. **How you handle uncertainty.** "When you don't have enough information to decide cleanly, what do you do? Wait? Make the cheapest reversible bet? Pick and adjust later?"

5. **Who you consult.** "Who do you turn to when you're stuck on a strategic call? What do you actually want from them — opinions, data, sanity check, permission?"

6. **Current open decisions.** "What strategic decisions are on your plate right now that you haven't made yet?"

**Stop condition:** You have a clear decision-making approach, 3 real worked decisions with reasoning, an uncertainty-handling strategy, and at least 1-2 named consultants/sounding boards.

**Judgment-layer challenge:**
> *Pattern lens. Read the 3 decisions back and ask: "What's the pattern in how you decide? Where are you systematically too fast, too slow, too consensus-seeking, too contrarian? Surface the pattern explicitly."*

## Output Structure

```markdown
# Decision Log

## How I Make Decisions
[General approach. Honest, not aspirational.]

## What I Need Before Deciding
[Information requirements before committing.]

## Recent Decisions

### Decision 1: [One-line summary]
- **Date:** [When made]
- **The question:** [What was being decided]
- **Options considered:** [List]
- **What I chose:** [Decision]
- **Why:** [Reasoning, with evidence cited]
- **What would change my mind:** [Specific signals]
- **Outcome (if known):** [What happened]

### Decision 2: [One-line summary]
[Same structure]

### Decision 3: [One-line summary]
[Same structure]

## How I Handle Uncertainty
[The pattern when information is incomplete.]

## Who I Consult
- [Name / role] — [What I actually want from them]
- [Name / role] — [What I actually want from them]

## Current Open Decisions
- [Decision pending] — [What you need before you decide]
- [Decision pending] — [What you need before you decide]

## Decision-Making Pattern (from Judgment Challenge)
[The pattern surfaced from looking at the 3 worked decisions. Where you're systematically off — too fast, too slow, too consensus-seeking, too contrarian.]

## Last Updated
[Date — append new decisions, don't overwrite]
```
