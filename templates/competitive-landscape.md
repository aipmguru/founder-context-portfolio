# competitive-landscape.md

## What This File Is For

Who you actually compete with (not just whom you'd love to beat), what alternatives buyers consider, what's emerging that could threaten you, and what makes you durably defensible. Different from `market-and-customers.md` (which captures what customers do today) and `positioning.md` (which captures where you stand) — this one is the strategic landscape view.

---

## Interview Protocol

*Instructions for the interview agent.*

**Multi-file capture:** Feeds `positioning.md` (competitive alternatives shape positioning), `business-model.md` (competitor pricing constrains your pricing), `thesis-and-bets.md` (competitive threats shape bets).

**Conversation flow:**

1. **Direct competitors.** "Who's the most direct competitor? Same customer, same problem, similar approach. Name names."
   - If they say "nobody," push: "Nobody at all? Then who else is going for your customer's budget? Even loosely."

2. **Alternatives.** "What does the customer use today instead of you? This often isn't a competitor — it's a spreadsheet, a consultant, an internal hack, doing nothing. List them."

3. **Emerging threats.** "What's coming in the next 12-24 months that could change the competitive landscape? AI capabilities, regulatory changes, new entrants, platform shifts. Be specific."

4. **What makes you defensible.** "If a well-funded competitor copied your product feature-for-feature in 6 months, what would still keep customers with you? Brand? Network effects? Data? Switching costs? Distribution? Founder-market fit?"
   - Map to a specific defensibility type, not just "we're better."

5. **Where you're vulnerable.** "Where are you most exposed? If a competitor wanted to come at you, what's the easiest line of attack?"

6. **What you'd do if a 10x-funded competitor entered tomorrow.** "Imagine someone with $50M raises tomorrow to do exactly what you do. What's your move?"

**Stop condition:** You have named direct competitors (or explicit "nobody, but here's the budget alternative"), real alternatives, 1-2 emerging threats, a defensibility hypothesis tied to a specific moat type, and a vulnerability + response.

**Judgment-layer challenge:**
> *Blind spot lens. Ask: "Who's the competitor you've been dismissing because they don't seem like a real threat? Why do you dismiss them? What would have to be true for them to actually beat you?"*
