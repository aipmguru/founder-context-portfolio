# market-and-customers.md

## What This File Is For

Who your customer actually is, who your beachhead is (the wedge customer that opens the bigger market), what language they use when they talk about their pain, and what they're using today instead of you. Behavior-based, not demographic.

---

## Interview Protocol

*Instructions for the interview agent.*

**Multi-file capture:** Feeds `competitive-landscape.md` (alternatives), `positioning.md` (target customer), and `business-model.md` (willingness to pay).

**Conversation flow:**

1. **The customer in one sentence.** "Who's your customer? Be specific. Not 'small businesses.' Not 'PMs.' One sentence with a real human in mind."
   - If they say "everyone," push: "Pick one. Who's the one type of customer you'd cry if they churned tomorrow?"

2. **The beachhead.** "Of all the customers you could serve, who's the *first* one whose pain is so acute they'd buy a half-built version tomorrow? That's your beachhead. Why them?"
   - If vague, push: "What's the specific moment of pain that makes them open their wallet? Describe a Tuesday morning where they hit it."

3. **What they actually do today.** "When this customer has the pain you solve — what do they do today? Not 'nothing.' They're doing *something*. Spreadsheet? A consultant? A workaround? A different tool?"
   - This is your real competition. Capture it specifically.

4. **The customer language bank.** "What words do they use when they describe the problem? Not the words you use as the founder. The exact phrases they say when they're frustrated. Give me 3-5."
   - If they only have founder-language, push: "Have you talked to a real customer in the last 30 days? What did they actually say? If not, that's an open question we should log."

5. **Who's NOT your customer.** "Who looks like a fit but isn't? The ones you'd have to talk yourself into. Why aren't they?"

**Stop condition:** You have a specific beachhead, the specific pain, what they do today, real customer language (or an explicit gap noted), and a clear "not-customer" boundary.

**Judgment-layer challenge:**
> *Pre-mortem lens. Ask: "Twelve months from now, this beachhead turned out to be wrong. What would the signal have been? Who would the right beachhead have been instead?"*
